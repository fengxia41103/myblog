I lost Freddie yesterday. It was a tough day, and a tough
decision. He woke me up by a seizure at maybe 11:30pm. Took him
15min-ish to calm down, with multiple waves of minor seizures after
the first major one. It was, sad.

Took him to ER. Doc suggested putting him to sleep as an option, and
looked grave. My mind went, not blank, but slowed tremendously. I felt
it stood still. I couldn't process the message, though I understood
clearly each word she was saying. It was too sudden for me. Freddie
was extra sleepy on that morning. When I met Noah and his mom for
dinner, I actually mentioned this to her, and what surprised me was
finding him standing in the middle of the room when I opened the door
&mdash; he looked usual, looked lost. Taking him to bed lying next to
me, I thought time, if given a few more days of passing this storm,
that he would have recovered, with his good appetite and happiness.

But, it didn't. Signed the paper. Noah's mom came over. We patted him,
waiting for the moment. Then, he raised his head a bit, looked alert,
somewhat, and his big ears were flying, from time to time, and, I saw
his eyes. Maybe, maybe. Give him another try, at least, maybe, there
is a chance, and a miracle? I signed him home at 6am the next
morning. Slept on the couch in ER next to him. It was a cold room. It
was a cold night.

With all the hopes and prays, he slept, pee-ed in bed, got him up and
gave a brief bath, he was weak but stood. I guess I was really seeking
all the signs that looked promising, positive, hopeful &mdash; he
pee-ed, he stood, his eyes opened, even his vomit of the milk I gave
him earlier &larr; these are all good signs, right!!!?

Then, two seizures stroke, one at 11:30 and one at 1:45pm. The second
one looked, heartbreaking. Holding him in my arms, I didn't know what
to do anymore. I didn't want him to go through another such
anymore. Called the ER, took him back there, and. It was hard. It is
hard. I never had a dog in my life, and he just dropped into my life
because he had nowhere to go. I didn't treat him well cause I didn't
know how, and I didn't understand well of him, either. I still don't
know how to take care of him well. But, at least, in recent days and
weeks, he had good meals, his bones, his furs became well and shiny,
much less skin issues, and he was even becoming picky on his old
favourate foods such as milk &mdash; it used to be bottom up style,
now, I should say, then, he would only take some, to his own
satisfaction, and left the rest in the bowl! "Spoiled!" I sometimes
thought. But I was glad to see him doing well &mdash; well well, who
knows how many more days he would still have.

Now I know. There were not many days left.

Very sorry Friddie. I miss you. I didn't expect you would be leaving
me such a deep feeling when you were around. Sometimes I didn't even
think you were handsome or cute or being a emotional support like
Bunny. But, you did, you were. Your last picture, the eye, I love it,
it's an eye of a youth, a little boy, that was born to this world with
a cute face, a sweet temper, a quiet personality, and a golden
heart.

I love you Friddie. I miss you. I'm blessed that you came to my
life. Five years had been too short. Your life had been too short. I
always joked that you are a 15-year-old old old man. You were. But you
grew old with a grace, retained your tender self. I always complained
that your photos couldn't see the eyes cause they were all hidden in
the black hairs. But, whenever I caught a eclipse of them, it had such
a playful boyish spark. It melts my heart.

Be good there. I'll bring you home in about a week. I'm gonna put you
on the shelf, where there is a lot of sunshine, warm, close to
music. And don't forget to sing along, when there is a piano song.

Come to my dream sometimes. I'll miss you very very much.
