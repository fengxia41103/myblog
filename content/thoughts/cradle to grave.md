I just had a thought this morning. I think I wrote in an article
before that software engineer will become the most popular person in
the marriage market, not because that they earn much, but because, as
more and more aspect of your life is being _driven_, or I should say,
_determined_ by software's logic, thus, the software engineer as a
whole, will become more and more **powerful** in the society, not in
the sense of status of power, but in the practical sense that, these
software behaviors &mdash; the `if this, then that` code path, is
literally then defining a **decision process**, which has a material
impact on this real world. But how did that guy/girl behind the
keyboard wrote that `if..else`!? I mean, as a software engineer
myself, how many of such statement was put in **completely on my whim
of wisdom at that particular moment**!? A LOT! And even worse, we all
know too well that I could hardly understand my own code after a few
weeks, which only testifies that the _decision process I codified_ is
such a _light bulb moment_ (putting it in a nice way!) that even its
own author can't really much rationale it in a spanned time frame.

What does it mean then? It means a few surprises!

First of all, needless to say, we have all heard that 这个世界是个巨大
的草台班子. What this is really saying is that, at least in the arena
of human decision making, including, and perhaps in particular, in a
system, such as the government, which had been expected to be
_systematic_, _thought through_, _supported by facts/proof_, or even
worse, _being "scientific"_, is a disappointment in reality. So why?
Why would a decision process that had been supposed to be predictable,
accurate, precise and so on such descriptions, **did not realize**!?
What's the missing? And of course, one should also question, is this
conclusion itself, true? Hard to prove true or false. But from many
people's personal perception, at least part of this statement is
TRUE. The hard part is to analyze the causal logic. Human are
certainly trying, as in a scientific research, to change a factor, and
many times change too many a factors at the same time, in the opposite
of a controlled experiment**, thus feeling more like a blind mouse
exploring a maze &mdash; randomly turning right and/or left, runs to a
dead end, try again, cluelessly. A king's system didn't work out, well,
let's try a king-less one, and so on. Where are we now then? Every so
often we think we found a recipe &mdash; communism, democracy, the
United States of America.. and yet, Nazi is walking in broad daylight
on the street in America today, and ICE is _arresting_ people (well, I
can't hardly even use this word here as ICE and its backings are
certainly don't see these human beings as "people" at all.. they are
viewed as a dirt you want to brush off your shining shoe) while the
children of these "people" were left crying and screaming and helpless
and hopeless.

So, why!? Can a software engineer type of thinking fix this broken
trial-and-error? I'm pessimistic. Each of these decision makers are
exactly like the software engineers I described &mdash; they made up
some `if-else` and put it in action. When there are so many `if-else`
chained up, it's a math problem, `2^N`, as simple as that, and it will
overwhelm any human being's brain! Just how many possibilities are
there, and how did you **test**!? If a paid QA can't even bother to
exhaust code path of an application, let alone a society. Thus, random
decision path were put in by whoever had the keyboard, at their whim
of _wisdom_ at a light bulb moment, and they themselves wont' even
recognize after a few weeks. I have a word for this, **decision-making
dementia**.

Secondly, if software, aks. system, is driving more and more of our
life, it works in favor of an omnipotent government, like the North
Korea and its big or small brothers and copycats. In modern society
(or as many think that's where we are in), the foundation (or I should
say, the mainstream thinking at this moment 10/11/2025) of it is the
more and more refined division of duty &mdash; profession of A, B, C,
D... and coordination in some mass scale creates a scalability effect,
and that's how human bubbled up over all other beings (well, let's not
brag about this in front of virus, bacteria, or even ants) on this
planet. However, this view calls for a boundary, aka. a finish line of
your duty, over which you pass the torch onto someone else. This view
then leads to the thought of democracy, individualism, one person one
vote, social contract, I don't need priest to talk to God, freedom of
`<plug in a word here!>`.

This echos our life experience raising our kid, the most heroic
endeavor most of us may ever have in our life time &mdash; not only in
the context of continuing the human race, but as the single
effort/work you persist in the sheer amount of time, volume of labor,
money, emotion... you name it. The kid grows to 18, gets into college,
leaves home, becoming **independent**. Would parents stop worry & care
for them after this boundary? Of course. But it is not a requirement,
and everyone gets a sense of relieve though it's a mixed feeling of 不
舍 and responsibility-free.

But, this same pattern works exactly the opposite in software
engineering! Many of engineers think, mostly led by the expectation
set by their job description or title, that, "I'm a developer, you are
a QA, I throw my work over the wall, now it's your problem", thus
there is still a boundary where my job finishes, and yours
starts. **However, the application, as long as it's in production,
it's a never ending care!** It's like a never-grow-to-18 baby, you,
the software engineers, are tasked to feed this baby from **cradle to
grave**!

Therefore, when our life, our society, are more and more being
determined, or influenced at least (and in today's AI era, even in an
no-one-knows-how-it-gets-that-answer way) by software, it means **the
sentiment of caring from cradle to grave** will become more and more
exercised, accepted, and louder. Like an idea planted in someone's
head, that seed will grow, and can get to a size that literally casts
a shadow by blocking the Sun).

And this, is the thought I just had. I used to think introducing more
and more software and its deterministic nature to the ad-hoc decision
making is a good thing. Well, now I am having a 2nd thought.
